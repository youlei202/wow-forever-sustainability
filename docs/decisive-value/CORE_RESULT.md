# Candidate core answer: partner compensation and real task gains under fixed caps

**Established result: in a two-slot model class retaining all old/new crosses, fixed power caps, and legacy-source usefulness, a shared weak partner can support successive positive-gain rewards; three equipment releases constructed in advance from native component responses pass P/N/D/L/H/C/G together on the empirical means of one independent native confirmation. The statistical claim of a first-release gain above 1% remains unresolved, and mathematical originality has not been established.**

1. **What exactly does the player gain?** With equipment selected before combat and both sides allowed to reoptimize over all allowed equipment and three policies, the first release improves mean damage in the 180-second high-armor fight; the second improves the 10-second and 30-second damage windows; the third improves the same high-armor task again. Representative independent-sample gains are 1.0138%, 1.3497%, and 2.1811% of the fixed initial scale, respectively. These are empirical gains that remain unattainable after reoptimizing the old library, not reweightings of damage logs.
2. **What structure limits power?** Each component has four physical-amplitude features obtained by native calibration. A fixed set of 24 task×policy capability inequalities determines which complete crosses are admitted. In the first release, the new main hand exceeds the cap with the strong old off hand, but gives no gain with the other old off hand; the new off hand compensates for the former's power and leaves a gain in the high-armor task. The fixed-policy four-cell difference is approximately zero, so this joint necessity does not depend on positive damage interaction.
3. **Are legacy sources and complete crosses preserved?** Confirmation runs all 25 final physical combinations with all three policies and eight tasks; the prefrozen rule admits 23 combinations and rejects 2 that exceed the cap, with no item exceptions. All four initial configurations and every previously admitted configuration remain available, and all ten final sources have near-optimal uses. Under the original 5% near-optimal criterion, the minimum loadout count is K=1 in every release. This K does not mean that one loadout achieves every new optimal gain, or that all items must be carried.
4. **What determines capacity, locality, and complexity?** Theory gives a positive construction in the same additive two-slot model class: under fixed headroom h, legacy-use tolerance e≥h, positive gain g per release, monotone two-damage-channel distance δ, and the other assumptions in THEORY Section 6, the attainable release count is `min(floor(h/g),floor(1/δ))`, using one rule, a shared weak partner, and K=1. The native study completes three releases using 24 fixed rules, four features, and zero exceptions; this is not called maximum capacity. Positive gain still consumes fixed headroom in general. No native history-branch separation matched on current gain/headroom was found, nor any native repair cascade.
5. **What is actually proved, rather than merely renaming existing tools?** Section 6 of THEORY.md proves a complete class-level bound-attaining construction: the same adjustable partner structure simultaneously gives first-release individual infeasibility, joint feasibility, checks of all old/new crosses, shared retention of legacy sources, positive G, D formed by actual two-damage-channel composition within the model, and fixed K (the native seven-dimensional D is checked separately). It does not merely prove that “different vectors exist.” Section 7 gives a quantitative counterexample for current-coverage and future-completion summary size under legacy-source retention, and exposes its `e−h` headroom restriction. The proofs are complete, but the tools and key intuitions are closely related to existing constrained composition, Pareto-safe replacement, packing, and potential functions; this round provides no evidence for presenting these propositions as new foundational mathematics. The reviewable new empirical result is the prediction and testing of these conditions on a previously unrun native equipment sequence.

## An executable design principle

Maintain each component's response contribution rather than relying only on a ranking of independently strongest equipment. For a new primary component, subtract its task×policy contribution from the fixed cap to obtain the remaining budget a partner must satisfy; then intersect it with the positive-gain lower bound for the target task. Continue partner design only if this completion set is nonempty. All old partners must also enter the search; existing substitutes cannot be hidden to manufacture joint necessity.

For the first release, old MH×old OH gives four configurations. Releasing the new MH alone or the new OH alone gives a maximum admitted gain of zero; releasing them together gives a gain in the high-armor task. Removing either new source returns the optimum to the complete old-library level. Among all nine complete combinations, only new MH2+old OH0 exceeds the cap. Increasing target armor changes the value of direct physical damage relative to Talon physical damage over time and Thunderfury Nature damage; this is actual engine resolution, not log reweighting. More precisely, weapon amplitudes also affect damage over time such as Deep Wounds, and native response coefficients measure the net effect.

The maximum absolute fixed-policy four-cell damage interaction across the three releases is 1.14e−13 DPS. This instance therefore rules out the explanation that “positive physical synergy is necessary for joint gain”; a fixed cap makes the feasible partner set depend on the component itself, which suffices to create release complementarity. Removing the admission cap makes unrestricted complete crosses violate P in all three releases. Replacing demand with eight copies of the same sustained fight makes all three releases fail prespecified G; task differences do have a role.

The second and third releases cannot both be described as “two mutually necessary items”: removing OH3 in the second release does not reduce optimal gain, and removing MH4 in the third does not reduce it either. They satisfy N/D when released jointly, but only the first release needs both items for G. Individual-item controls further distinguish their roles: in the second release, MH3 alone passes G but fails D, while OH3 alone passes D but fails G; in the third, OH4 alone passes G but fails D, while MH4 alone fails both D/G. No individual item in any of the three releases passes all seven criteria jointly, but batch necessity in the later two releases partly comes from original D and cannot be presented as each item contributing real gain. Six new sources do not imply six independent new uses. Preserve both zero deletion effects.

## Boundaries between class-level results and native evidence

The theoretical construction allows one weak partner to support multiple new primary components, without one-to-one repair. If a source has a retained configuration reaching `cap−relevance`, its usefulness is permanently protected under the future fixed cap. This sufficient condition depends only on the original numerical values and retention permissions; it does not assume that every future release has already passed L.

Of the ten final native sources, eight have the above empirical numerical witness; old OH1 and new OH3 do not. This round therefore verifies usefulness of all ten sources through these three releases, but cannot promise permanent usefulness of all ten under arbitrary later designs. Shared witnesses must actually be checked against legacy obligations; an abstract theorem cannot hide unmet assumptions.

In the abstract class with a single task, 5% headroom, 1% gain per release, and δ=5%, a five-release bound-attaining construction uses six new components, one admission rule, and K=1; all 12 final legal crosses are checked. It is an abstract result verified with exact rational arithmetic, adding zero native runs and zero class/race coverage. The three-release native result and five-release abstract construction are counted separately. The native eight-task nominal potential-function bound is 40 releases; the coarse bound computed from current confirmation means is 38 releases. Neither is an attainable release count, much less unlimited expansion.

## What the three competing directions yielded

- **Task demand:** Across four old ecologies, Human/Orc, and 392 development comparisons, 14 pass D but fail G, and 142 pass G but fail D. The original six criteria plus G have 32 passing rows. D and G must be reported separately. Development data come from earlier native tables with 16 seeds and are not counted as newly executed.
- **Complete combinations:** Across ten selected release/race cases, unrestricted natural composition passes 0/10; independent individual-item incremental budgets pass 0/10; same-dimensional generic single-row budgets and arbitrary-subset references both pass 6/10. There is no evidence that a structured algorithm outperforms generic solvers. The new four-feature model's 24-row rule also belongs to the generic linear-rule class.
- **Batches and histories:** Fixed-universe analysis has 927 method/release evaluations: 406 natural-composition, 406 full-power-safe, and 115 existing-subset evaluations; 86/406 full-power-safe releases satisfy both the original six criteria and G. Of 724 exact reachable-state DP attempts, 662 fail; the longest selected path is three releases, and no five-release path was found. No history-branch pair matched on current gain and headroom was found, so history-entanglement separation cannot be claimed.

The new construction search uses only five basis-amplitude anchors to form 24 response models, tested at two all-axis combination points and four old crosses; the maximum residual across 144 nonfit cells and 36,864 per-seed predictions is 6.82e−13. Of the subsequent 98,304 model candidates, 951 pass the design guard margins and joint screen, and three pairs are selected; 97,353 fail that guard screen, while 948 qualify but are not selected. The 98,301 “unselected” candidates are not all counted as infeasible. Mean predictions on the independent panel are not fits to the same per-seed data; the maximum cell-mean error is 6.2294 DPS, and the first-release gain margin does in fact shrink.

## Prior work and unfinished aspects

Convex coverage, constrained Pareto composition, and linear response under fixed control are established tools. Geilen et al.'s Pareto algebra explicitly requires dominance replacement to preserve constraints; “maximized reward is also bounded above” does not satisfy that safe-replacement condition. This study's weak-partner mechanism lies within that known boundary and cannot be claimed to refute coverage theory. Ludus already studies fixing old cards while designing new ones, so “preserving old parameters plus balancing all combinations” is not itself original either. See PRIOR_WORK_DELTA.md for detailed comparisons with the original papers.

This round delivers a candidate core answer with a positive construction, a complete class-level proof, unseen native validation, and explicit failure boundaries. Foundational theoretical novelty remains unestablished, and there is no guarantee of AISTATS Oral-level quality. There are no new generic phase scripts, additional class coverage, native repair cascades, or infinite-positive-gain conclusions. The engine's survival termination semantics do not support kill/survival claims; utility uses only damage over fixed combat windows.
