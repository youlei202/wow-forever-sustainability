# What the new native validation establishes

The new capability is a prospective, auditable completion query on an independently
registered finite equipment menu: it preserves the proposed publication, verifies
every activated pair and every source, and distinguishes a supported answer from
an exact sample-mean answer and from an unresolved statistical case. The strongest
new application finding is that preservation of **legacy** sources really blocks
otherwise possible updates across several new menus. This is broader evidence
than the original selected trinket case, but it is not evidence that the specialised
solver is faster or that difficult helper cascades are common.

The primary panel is 16 new metadata-selected menus from the same pinned native
database and executable, with 8 targets each. It covers four registered source
strata and four actually executed classes: Warrior, Paladin, Mage and Druid.
Alliance/Horde labels denote eight actual contexts, not eight independent
mechanisms. Item IDs can overlap older work; selection used metadata and not old
winning response cases. Each full physical table used a fresh fixed 16,384-seed
block. No timeout, missing cell or unfinished run was converted to a negative.

| Primary base-menu outcome | Queries / 128 |
|---|---:|
| Supported YES | 42 |
| Supported NO, all allowed helper counts exhausted | 76 |
| Statistical UNKNOWN | 10 |
| Exact fresh-mean YES / NO | 43 / 85 |
| Prediction answer changed on fresh means | 0 |
| Frozen positive witness supported | 38 / 43 positive predictions |
| Frozen positive witness valid on fresh means | 43 / 43 positive predictions |

These are descriptive counts of correlated targets in 16 registered menus. They
are neither independent binomial trials nor a prevalence estimate for all game
content. Population statements are conditional on the finite simultaneous event,
whose fixed-N paired-t/Bonferroni coverage is approximate, not distribution-free.
The new main allocation is .04, split .0025 per catalogue; .01 remains reserved.
The old campaign already used its own .05. Combining old and new main events has
nominal union allocation .09, not a renewed global .05 claim.

## The design issue is not merely that weak rewards exist

Among the 76 supported negative base queries, 69 admit a supported value-only
completion when all source-retention obligations are removed. The source-group
audit keeps cap, gain, targets, physical rows and optional helpers fixed:

| Retention obligation group | Group alone suffices to obstruct | Removing group restores supported YES |
|---|---:|---:|
| Legacy history H | 42 queries, 10 catalogues | 28 queries, 10 catalogues |
| Required targets D | 39 queries, 13 catalogues | 16 queries, 8 catalogues |
| Published helpers | 0 | 0 |

The columns mean sufficiency and necessity, respectively; labels can overlap and
must not be added. The legacy results rule out describing every retention-active
negative as an intrinsically weak target. Conversely, this panel does **not** show
that helper self-retention itself changes an answer. It remains necessary in the
mathematical contract and in correctness counterexamples, but a new empirical
mechanism claim about it is unsupported here.

The tolerance policy substantially changes these judgments. Reusing the original
finite event by valid interval implications, the supported YES/NO/UNKNOWN counts
at e=.005, .01, .02 and .05 are respectively 21/92/15, 42/76/10, 68/50/10 and
106/14/8. At 5%, 58 of the primary 76 negative queries become supported YES; 11
retention-essential negatives remain in four catalogues. Thus the evidence is
not threshold-invariant. The residual obstructions and the transparent tradeoff
are more defensible than a claim that most future updates are inherently blocked.

## Construction stability differs from answer stability

The 43 positive base answers all transfer to the fresh mean table, but five frozen
publications are not confidence-supported: one gain margin and four source-use
margins remain unresolved. Four of those queries have a different supported
publication. Those replacement witnesses are valid existence certificates, not
successful confirmations of the original proposed publication.

The expanded-menu panel makes this distinction sharper. Its 24 positive query
answers also transfer, but two of the original proposed publications fail the
fresh-mean retention check. Both are correlated targets in the Horde Warrior
validation-01 catalogue. They contain the optional a09 helper and leave old a00
with useful task mass zero on the fresh means. Alternative completions exist.
Only 18/24 expanded frozen witnesses receive confidence support; six remain
unsupported, including those two mean failures. This is a concrete caution for
update design: stable existence predictions do not guarantee that an arbitrary
published witness has adequate margins. It is an illustrative failure in one
catalogue, not a measured population failure rate or a new robustness algorithm.

## Challenges that did not establish the hoped-for effect

Eight prespecified menus added three low-item-level optional alternatives and
measured all new crosses. Across the 64 paired targets there are no mean or
confidence-state transitions: the mean states remain 40 NO and 24 YES; confidence
states remain 36 NO, 23 YES and 5 UNKNOWN. This campaign does not demonstrate a
candidate-expansion rescue. It does check that old certificates were recomputed
against every newly allowed helper rather than inherited without verification.

The four-task projection challenge produces five fresh mean NO-to-YES changes
when task indices [0,3] receive equal half weights. One was not predicted by its
development mean table. **None** of the five opposing answers is jointly supported
by the confidence bounds. This is an unresolved task-contract effect, not a
confirmed separation. The projection changes the task distribution and is not an
optional-candidate monotonicity test.

On the fresh base means, 19 of the 43 feasible targets require helpers; direct
publication finds 24, current-gain greedy 27, minimum-increment greedy 37,
weakest-source repair 43 and two-helper lookahead 43. All successes have exact
full-contract checks; heuristic failure is UNKNOWN. The two stronger simple
methods therefore find every feasible target in this panel. There is no evidence
here that a deep specialised search is required. Fair generic-solver timing and
repeated-query results must determine any computational advantage separately.

## What actually executed

Native action/resource/aura diagnostics, not just APL/source inspection, establish:

* Warrior Whirlwind, Bloodthirst and attack actions in all 428 measured cells.
* Paladin Command and Righteousness casts and both proc-damage channels in all
  428 cells; the preset UI option alone would not establish this.
* Druid Wrath, Moonfire, Insect Swarm and mana regeneration in all 428 cells;
  guarded Starfire executes in 214. Its source-code guard is not assumed to fire
  in the other cells, and execution does not establish its causal contribution.
* Mage Second Wind activation and actual mana gain in 46/46 equipped cells,
  Blue Dragon aura uptime in 16/16, Emblem in 38/38, Talisman in 18/18 and Burst
  of Knowledge in 38/38. Fire Ruby activates in 46/46 but yields actual mana in
  23/46. Charm activation alone does not prove that its arcane buff contributes
  under the fixed policy.

These are four source/response structures under a fixed engine and fixed policy,
not comprehensive live-game mechanic validation. Database faction equipment
checks do not establish every live acquisition rule; faction-named acquisition
items without encoded restrictions remain explicitly acquisition-unverified.

## Manuscript relevance

The strongest body-level addition is a **prospective completion audit with
attributed source obligations**: it can confirm a construction, exhaust all
allowed repairs, show which preservation requirement is responsible, and expose
when a previously selected construction is too fragile. The independent native
panel and tolerance challenge substantiate that capability beyond a selected
example. This is supporting evidence for the completion problem and certificate
workflow, not a new claim of algorithmic superiority or broad ecological
prevalence. The two failed expanded constructions are useful negative evidence.

The next native research question should concern robust witness selection and
margin-aware publication, rather than finding more release dates, counting more
races, or forcing a candidate-expansion flip. Any new method for that question
needs its own prospective comparison against generic robust optimisation.
