# R3 — Reusable rules hold; sustained novelty remains unestablished

1. **Is Blood Talon–Thunderfury an isolated case or a family?** In the existing item-ID map, this remains the only pair meeting the original behavioral-difference threshold; changing weapon parameters within the real engine, however, expands it into a reproducible compensation family. Under a 5% cap, four interior parameter points × Human/Orc give 8 conditions, each with interval support for power and competitiveness of 4 Thunderfury loadouts. Behavioral difference D remains an empirical-mean result. The original Human canonical loadout itself is not confirmed safe.
2. **What mechanism predicts complementarity?** Weakening or changing main-hand physical damage and speed leaves room for an off-hand magical-damage channel that previously exceeded the cap. All eight main proc-toggle contrasts lie within the predeclared interaction-tolerance interval of ±1% of initial DPS; no large positive proc amplification is found.
3. **Does complexity grow with item count or type count?** Simple type count is not the correct variable. The reusable object is a fixed event-control mechanism and its power envelope. The current rule originally has 288 conditions; removing numerical redundancy leaves 12 power constraints, 24 fixed-configuration branches, and 4 parameter-domain boundaries, with compact JSON totaling 3,408 bytes. With these configurations fixed, increasing new parameterized items from 4→32 does not add rules; with 8 items fixed but speed mechanisms increasing from 1→3, compressed constraints increase from 4→12. This curve measures the number of control mechanisms, not new semantic types.
4. **Can frozen rules transfer?** For 32 unseen parameterized items under the same random-seed coupling, the maximum per-seed error across 3,072 native results is 3.41e−13 DPS; type rules, a same-feature generic method, and a finite-safe-set reference agree exactly, admitting 197/256 loadouts. Coefficients fail when speed, proc rate, or rage mechanisms change, and unknown configurations should be rejected. Independent-seed means have substantial errors; algebraic exactness cannot be treated as a statistical safety certificate.
5. **Does complementary selection extend sustainable expansion over 20 waves?** Evidence is insufficient. Across two candidate pools × two races, the four conditions have successful prefixes [0, 0, 1, 1], and 0/4 pass all 20 waves; only 4/80 waves are accepted and 76/80 rejected, without replacement draws. Complementary, same-information generic optimization, and repricable scalar methods have identical trajectories. New items can remain useful, but novel behavior is quickly exhausted, and Human is also constrained by legacy-source competitiveness.
6. **What is the strongest theory?** Under conditions including event trajectories independent of reward amplitude, affine rewards, finite context envelopes, and strict safety/competitiveness/behavioral margins, a fixed-complexity rule permits a nonempty complementary parameter neighborhood. Capacity for positively separated behavior is finite; low rank or finitely many type names alone do not control constraint count. This reward decomposition is consistent with existing successor-feature ideas and is not presented as an original theorem.
7. **Is this sufficient to support a central AISTATS 2027 claim?** **Not yet.** There is solid evidence of a native mechanism case, transfer boundaries, and failure of sustained novelty; sustained new value across mechanism families and multiple waves has not been demonstrated, nor is there evidence of superiority over same-information generic or scalar optimization.
